To determine whether honey is 100% natural, you need to measure the carbon isotope ratio of C4 sugar in the honey. But what exactly is the C4 sugar index? Let's find out with Monabee._

## The C4 sugar index

Both C3 and C4 sugars are the two main types of sugar found in honey, but the process that creates them is different. C3 sugar is created through the photosynthesis of C3 plants and is the main type of sugar, making up to 99% of honey. C4 sugar, meanwhile, is created through the photosynthesis of C4 plants, mostly from sugarcane, corn, and some other tropical plants.

If honey has a C4 sugar content below 7%, it's called pure honey. Conversely, if honey has a C4 sugar content higher than 7%, it may have been blended with sugar from other plant sources.

In fact, many Vietnamese food regulatory agencies require businesses to test the amount of cane sugar (sucrose) in honey as a basis for determining whether honey is purely natural, but this is almost meaningless. Beekeepers don't mix cane sugar directly into honey—instead, they feed bees sugar around the time of harvesting to increase yield. In that case, the bees' digestive system has already broken the sugar down into two simple sugars, glucose and fructose (the two sweet substances found in natural honey). So no matter how many tests you run, you won't find cane sugar in the honey.

Most countries in Europe and the US identify substandard honey by testing its C4 sugar index. The C4 sugar index allows regulatory agencies to trace the origin of the glucose and fructose sweeteners, and the C4 sugar index reflects this honestly and accurately.

The permitted index for honey is C4 sugar < 7%. If C4 sugar > 7%, that honey is considered to have been produced from bees fed sugar.

This shows that the smaller the C4 sugar index, the higher-quality the honey. A C4 sugar index of 0 or approximately 0 is called natural honey, similar to wild honey, even if it's farmed honey.
