## How is honey made?

A **honey bee** colony consists of 3 types: **the queen bee, drones, and worker bees**, each with a different role. The queen bee lays eggs and maintains order within the colony, drones fertilize the queen, and worker bees build the hive, care for the young larvae, and forage for food. So honey comes into being thanks to the hard, tireless labor of the worker bees.

To gather these precious drops of nectar from the earth and sky, worker bees use their long tongues to collect tiny droplets of nectar from flowers, leaves, buds, and plant sap. Bees are quite special in that they have two stomachs — one for digesting food, and one for storing nectar, which can hold up to 70mg of nectar, equivalent to their own body weight.

After gathering enough nectar, the worker bee returns to the hive and passes the nectar to another worker bee waiting at home. The bees at home then take the nectar into their mouths and work it there for about half an hour, converting complex sugars into simple sugars. It's then stored in compartments inside the hive, and the bees continue fanning their wings to dry the honey further, before sealing the compartments with beeswax to store it for later consumption.

## What is pure wild forest honey?

**Pure wild forest honey** is honey taken from natural beehives in the forest. These hives are often built over a very long time, which is why the honey tends to taste better and more fragrant.

However, harvesting **wild forest honey** using traditional methods is extremely difficult and dangerous, since wild bee colonies often build their hives on cliffsides or high up in trees. What's more, harvesting wild honey usually requires destroying the hive, or even killing the bee colony. This method is not only dangerous but also depletes natural resources.
