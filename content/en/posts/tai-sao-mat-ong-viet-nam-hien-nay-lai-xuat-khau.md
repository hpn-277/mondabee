# ****Why Is Vietnamese Honey Currently Underperforming in Exports?****

 

## ****Today, most Vietnamese honey has a high moisture content and ferments easily due to improper harvesting and beekeeping practices.****

![](/images/posts/tai-sao-mat-ong-viet-nam-hien-nay-lai-xuat-khau/Giai-ma-giac-mo-thay-ong-lam-to-danh-con-gi-3.jpg)

Although [Vietnamese honey](http://cafef.vn/mat-ong.html) meets the technical requirements under strict management and monitoring, [chemical residue](http://cafef.vn/du-luong-hoa-chat.html) levels have been permitted for import into the EU market since 2013. However, to this day the EU market still accounts for only a small share of Vietnam's total honey exports to the world.

Many experts, both domestic and international, have pointed out the shortcomings that need to be addressed in Vietnamese honey, at the seminar "Maintaining and Expanding the Market for Vietnamese Honey Products Exported to the EU," organized by the Vietnam SPS Office on November 1 in Ho Chi Minh City, with support from the European Trade Policy and Investment Support Project (EU-MUTRAP).

As of September 15, 2017, Vietnam's honey exports for the year had reached about 29,000 tons, of which only 1,469 tons went to the EU market. According to analysis by Mr. Le Thanh Hoa, Deputy Director of the Vietnam SPS Office, demand for honey imports from EU partners is very large, offering many opportunities for market expansion for businesses and import-export companies.

However, the issue is that Vietnamese honey still faces a number of quality management barriers when entering the EU market. In particular, it must strictly follow proper technical processes in harvesting and processing, and comply with market regulations as well as importer requirements.

According to Mr. Hoa, to promote and help honey-related Vietnamese products penetrate deeper into the EU market, the focus needs to be on improving beekeeping processes and thorough preservation and packaging methods.

Accordingly, businesses are the key factor in raising knowledge and awareness in building brand image, promoting products, and elevating the Vietnamese honey brand.

Mr. Nicolaus Bieger, an international expert with the EU-MUTRAP Project, said Vietnam needs to address quality challenges in honey production and processing, including: moisture content, yeast, feed residue, and color.

At the same time, production and business units and import-export companies need to quickly overcome their lack of understanding of quality and market trends, such as the growing preference for green, clean, health-safe, and organic product lines, in order to better meet the needs of consumers in the EU market.

Mr. Nicolaus noted that Vietnamese businesses have made the "mistake" of harvesting honey too early, or while it is still immature, resulting in a high moisture content that easily leads to fermentation, greatly affecting the quality of the honey.

In addition, beehive boxes are often too small; a larger space needs to be created for the bee colony to produce honey. With more room, the colony will grow, and bees that are more comfortable will produce more honey of better quality. At the same time, a more spacious hive allows for better airflow, helping the honey dry out more and reducing its moisture content.
